My admiration for SERPRO, Brazil's federal data processing company, started in an auditorium seat, in the first semesters of my Bachelor's in Information Systems at the Instituto Federal Fluminense, in Campos dos Goytacazes. It took me more than a decade to turn that admiration into a badge.

Along the way there were attempts, years standing still, a long wait and a plan B. Throughout the text I share what I wish someone had told me earlier.

## A talk about cryptography

In college I attended a seminar where a SERPRO employee gave a talk about cryptography. I don't remember his name, but I remember the feeling of finding it amazing. It was the first time I realized there was a company inside the government solving serious technology problems at a national scale.

I already wanted to work in the public sector. After that talk, and everything I read about the company afterwards, my interest in SERPRO was inevitable.

## The first exams and choosing UFF

In Brazil, most public sector jobs are filled through competitive exams called *concursos*. Right after college I took a batch of them and passed three: IT Technician at the Campos dos Goytacazes City Hall, IT Analyst at UERJ and IT Technician at UFF. I also took the MPU and Banco do Brasil exams around the same time, without passing.

I chose UFF because it was the only federal one of the three. I took office at the end of 2012, moved from Campos to Niterói and began a phase that would last 14 years.

## My attempts at SERPRO (and the ones I skipped)

Between 2012 and 2023, SERPRO opened few exams:

| Year | Exam | What I did |
|------|------|------------|
| 2013 | National (Cespe), 360 openings for Analyst and Technician, plus a reserve list | Took it, didn't pass |
| 2013 | Belo Horizonte regional, for Analyst and Technician in Minas Gerais | Skipped it: I didn't want to leave the Southeast |
| 2021 | Cebraspe, 165 Analyst openings for temporary contracts | Skipped it |
| 2023 | Cebraspe, Analyst (Technology specialization), 602 immediate openings plus a reserve list | Passed |

I took the 2013 exam on short notice. I had just taken office at UFF and moved to a new city, and I was adjusting to everything at once. There was no way to prepare.

In 2021 the temporary contract weighed against it. I had job security at UFF and already had a son, and trading that for a contract with an end date made no sense.

Between 2013 and 2021 SERPRO went eight years without an exam for permanent positions. If you're waiting for the "next exam notice" from some agency, know that gaps like this happen.

## The years standing still

Job security at UFF made me comfortable. For years I simply stopped studying for exams.

What pulled me out of it was my son, Pedro Israel, born in 2019. From then on I started studying again. In 2023, the year the SERPRO exam notice came out, my daughter Ana Lis was born, and I was more motivated than ever.

## Learning how to study (for real)

My first passes came with no structure at all. Looking back, I didn't know how to study. To get back in the game after so long, I had to learn a method. I started studying through practice questions instead of only reading theory, using study cycles so I didn't depend on a rigid schedule by weekday, and doing spaced repetition so the content wouldn't evaporate.

I used Gran Concursos for theory, TecConcursos for practice questions and PCI Concursos to follow exam notices and news.

With a job and two small kids, the routine I could manage was one hour a day. When news started coming out that the SERPRO exam was planned, I went up to about three hours a day. In practice, I studied almost only after the notice was published.

## The 2023 exam

The exam had three tests: multiple choice and an essay on the same day, and a hands-on programming test on another day.

I didn't do very well in the first phase. But I nearly aced the programming test, and that's what pulled my ranking up.

The result was the reserve list, in position 1125 of the general list, in an exam with 602 immediate openings. I was satisfied. It was a very competitive exam, I was coming from years without studying seriously, and I had prepared almost only after the notice.

> Tip for anyone taking the next SERPRO exam: study a lot through practice questions and study programming. In 2023 the programming test covered Java (widely used at SERPRO), Python and JavaScript. Focus on algorithms, data structures, object orientation and logic. A good programming test can make up for an average first phase, as it did for me.

## The wait and the Telegram community

After the results were confirmed, the phase every reserve list candidate knows began: the wait.

The approved candidates built a Telegram community that became the main source of information. News, rumors and calls all circulated there. There were rooms for several SERPRO topics and one room for each group of people called. For anyone waiting, a community like this makes a big difference, both to stay informed and to avoid going crazy alone.

Calls reached roughly position 925 of the general list. Considering the 20% of openings reserved for quota candidates, there were about 250 calls left before my turn.

Then the news and rumors stopped. Since it was an election year, I had already convinced myself nothing else would come.

## Plan B: the Rio de Janeiro Court of Justice

Meanwhile, I took the exam of the Rio de Janeiro Court of Justice (TJ RJ) for Judicial Analyst (Information Technology, Data Engineer). The career interested me and worked as a plan B in case SERPRO didn't call. It was also the court's first exam focused on data, an area I really like, and I bet it would be less competitive.

The exam was much harder than SERPRO's, more theoretical and more tiring. The law section weighed heavily on IT people. As is common with courts, there were no immediate openings. The court planned a reserve list of up to 40 candidates, but the exam was so hard that only 23 qualified. I ranked 16th on the general list, a much better result than at SERPRO, because this time I was much better prepared.

## The surprise: 310 more openings

On July 21, 2026, right after the TJ RJ final results, the news came: SERPRO published an amendment adding 310 openings, and it reached me.

I had already convinced myself I wouldn't be called, so I was overjoyed. I was called in September 2026 and started work on October 14, 2026.

My family celebrated as if the job were theirs, and their support carried me through the whole preparation.

## Saying goodbye to UFF after 14 years

I did this whole journey in complete silence: going back to studying, taking the exams, waiting and being called. Only my family knew.

My colleagues and managers at UFF only found out when I announced I had already filed my resignation. Everyone was surprised, because they thought I would stay there until I retired. My philosophy is simple: what nobody knows about, nobody can give opinions on or sabotage.

At UFF I worked as an IT technician at the journal *Online Brazilian Journal of Nursing*, where I migrated the systems to AWS and took care of OJS, including plugins and automation scripts. I also worked at the School of Nursing, with the computer lab, the websites of the School and its graduate programs, live streams and training.

Leaving took three steps: the resignation request through SEI, the government's electronic process system; returning the UFF laptop I had; and handing over files, scripts and processes to the colleagues who would take over my duties.

> Tip: document and hand over what you maintain before you leave. Scripts and processes that only exist in your head (or on your machine) become a problem for whoever stays.

Yes, I had butterflies in my stomach. Trading the security of a statutory civil servant for the private-sector labor regime of a state-owned company is not a trivial decision. But it's behind me now.

From UFF I take good friendships and a fondness for the place where I worked the longest in my life. The final goodbye is still to come: my former colleagues invited me to the end-of-year party in December.

## First day, team and adjusting

The first day was a welcome for new employees at SERPRO's Rio de Janeiro office, in the Horto neighborhood. There was a big coffee break in the morning and conversations with long-time employees, who told the company's history and their own experiences. We were very well received. Right there we got our work equipment: a top-of-the-line laptop, brand new in the box.

I joined the Innovation and Research Division for Personal Data Protection and Cyber Anti-Fraud. The team is small: my manager, me and two other analysts, also called through the same amendment, a month before me.

The biggest difference from UFF is the reach of the work. At the university, what I did basically reached the building where I worked. At SERPRO, it goes into technologies used across the country. The work model changed too: at UFF it was hybrid, mostly on-site, and at SERPRO I'm 100% remote.

## What now?

A month later, the admiration that started in a cryptography talk has been confirmed. I've never felt so fulfilled at work.

The TJ RJ is still on the horizon. If that call comes, I'll have a hard decision ahead: on one side, a better-paying career; on the other, a company that works with a huge variety of technologies. But that's a topic for a future post, if and when the court calls.

## Summary for anyone on the road

- Job security can turn into complacency. If you have a bigger goal, don't let comfort put it out.
- Learn how to study before studying more: practice questions, study cycles and spaced repetition.
- One hour a day is much more than zero. Increase it as the exam notice approaches.
- At SERPRO, the programming test decides. Practice algorithms, data structures and object orientation in Java and Python.
- A reserve list is not a defeat. Extra openings do happen.
- Have a plan B and, if possible, make it something you also enjoy.
- Find your community. Groups of approved candidates help you get through the wait.
- Take care of your exit: formal resignation, returning equipment and handing over knowledge.

If you're studying for SERPRO or going through the same wait, reach out on social media. I'd be glad to chat.
